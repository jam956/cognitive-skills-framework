# Around the circle, then a tilt

An archive holds a collection of fragile notebooks. Readers want to study them; handling is gradually damaging the pages. Some researchers need only the words. Others need to examine the binding, paper or marks that a reproduction may not preserve.

The opening question is how to make the collection useful while keeping it available for future readers. This imagined case illustrates the earlier Prism/Parallax search analogy. It is a worked description, not a recorded experiment.

## Walking around the same question

One position on the circle is easy to defend: open the notebooks to readers. A collection that cannot be studied has lost much of its purpose. From this position, restrictions look like the principal obstacle.

Across the circle, the opposite position is also coherent: restrict handling. A damaged page cannot be restored simply because access was well intentioned. From here, unrestricted use threatens the very thing readers came to study.

These are the thesis and antithesis. The movement between them brings different consequences into view while the subject remains the same collection. It does not require jumping to another discipline.

Walking farther around the circle may uncover other positions: different opening hours, a smaller reading room, stricter eligibility or shorter appointments. Each may change the amount of access. None yet changes the assumption that access means placing an original notebook in someone's hands.

Eventually the inquiry returns to the same tension. The collection must be used to fulfill its purpose, yet use can consume what makes it valuable. In the older vocabulary, this is a paradox position: a practical tension worth preserving, not proof that the problem is logically impossible.

## The tilt

The useful movement now is to reconsider what counts as access.

A reader tracing changes in vocabulary may need a searchable transcription and clear page images. A researcher studying a repair to the binding needs the original object. Both want access, but they are asking for different encounters with the collection.

That distinction supplies the missing dimension. Instead of moving only between more and less handling, the search can distinguish the kinds of use and the forms of access that serve them.

The earlier framework called this a **YZ tilt** or paradox escape. The picture is of tilting the plane in which the alternatives were arranged. The question stays in view, but the alternatives no longer have to be described entirely in the opening terms.

Here the subject remains access to the notebooks. The change is from one undifferentiated activity to several activities with different requirements. The tilt has not solved the problem merely by renaming it.

## Returning with something that can be tried

A candidate arrangement now becomes visible: provide reproductions for work they can adequately support, and retain supervised access to originals for questions that require the physical object.

This is a possible synthesis, not a halfway point between opening and closing the archive. It changes the arrangement by separating needs that had been treated as one. Preservation and use remain important; neither has been declared mistaken.

The arrangement still has to face the world. Reproductions may omit consequential details. Creating them also requires handling. Supervision costs time, and some access requirements may remain incompatible. Those facts can narrow the proposal or leave part of the tension unresolved.

The return path is the connection back to the opening question: does the arrangement actually allow meaningful study while protecting future access? A proposal that protects the notebooks by eliminating all useful study has lost one side of that question. A proposal that calls every reproduction equivalent to an original has lost another.

## Where the movements live now

The old analogy preceded the present division of responsibilities. The current package expresses related operations through several modules:

- [CDP](skills/CDP.md) keeps the selected domain and any materially useful additional lenses in view.
- [CTR](skills/CTR.md) names rotation, inversion and dimensional lift, along with what each operation must preserve.
- [ETE](skills/ETE.md) explores the resulting arrangements and retains alternatives.
- [PSPP](skills/PSPP.md) compares those arrangements against the evidence and constraints.
- [CREM](skills/CREM.md) can help when readers' and custodians' differing interests become the central relationship.
- [CEC](skills/CEC.md) can inspect a proposed sequence whose feasibility depends on permission, staffing or preparation.
- [DARE/IDCM](skills/DARE.md) can reopen the frame if an apparently neat solution keeps leaving important needs unexplained.

The current CTR definition's **dimensional lift** adds a missing dimension while preserving conflicting observations. It is a close functional counterpart to the tilt in this example. The complete historical circle is not implemented as one compulsory route in the published JSON.

## Keeping the analogy useful

The earlier Y and Z labels changed between discussions. The stable purpose was to re-express a difficult relationship while retaining the problem's identity and consequential constraints. These are conceptual search directions, not measured angles in an LLM's neural attention.

Sometimes walking around the circle is enough. Sometimes a tilt reveals a distinction worth testing. Sometimes no synthesis is supported and the honest result is a clearer account of the remaining conflict.
