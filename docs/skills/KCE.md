# KCE: preserve a thought while changing its level of generality

For the extended treatment, see [Chapter 8: KCE: Extracting a Reusable Concept](../chapters/08_KEY_CONCEPT_EXTRACTION.md).

**Question:** What can be carried into another situation without losing the relationship that made the original example useful?

## The problem and the method

A request to summarize can preserve the story but miss its reusable lesson. A request to generalize can produce a slogan that discards necessary conditions. KCE asks for a provisional governing concept, the relationships it preserves, and the limits of its applicability.

This makes the abstraction explicit enough to inspect and retrieve. A model can perform the operation through ordinary prompting; the skill supplies a repeatable contract for what must survive.

## Historical development

The [sentence-thought overview](../chapters/03_SENTENCE_THOUGHT_VECTORS.md#3-kce-elevate-the-concept-without-losing-the-thought) describes its role in moving from sentence-thought structure to reusable concepts. Later specifications retained several candidate anchors, reported compression risks and separated ontology, dynamics and regime rather than letting one attractive label settle the interpretation. Basin's consumer contract bounded elevation and preserved source support and failure boundaries.

The included v2.3 specification supersedes v2.2. Its source name is *Key Concept Extraction*; *Elevation* describes the abstraction task in the evaluation. These are related usages rather than two independently tested mechanisms.

## Example and boundary

A technician's ability to diagnose a pump does not grant access to a workshop controlled by someone else. A useful abstraction preserves capability, necessary access and control by another party. It does not license the claim that skill never produces independence.

Categories help locate the concept. Source support and a present-fit check determine whether its meaning applies. The [KCE evaluation](https://github.com/jam956/Key_Concept_Generalization_and_Specification_Eval) tests selected extraction and application abilities, with those tasks currently assessed separately.
