# Prism Coupler: bind the fields into a candidate system

Textbook chapter: [The Prism Coupler](../chapters/07_PRISM_COUPLER.md).

**Question:** Who does what, to whom, under which conditions?

## The problem and the method

Separating ontology, dynamics and regime helps inspection, but a list of fields is not yet a coherent situation. Roles can be misassigned, constraints attached to the wrong action, or shared dependencies overlooked. The Coupler binds these elements into actor-centered relationships.

Its contribution beyond a brief prompt is an explicit binding and handoff contract. It preserves alternatives when the evidence supports more than one interpretation.

## Historical development

April 20, 2026 discussions connected the emerging Dynamical Prism to constructing candidate entities and systems. By May 2, a Coupler route gate was being described for choosing relationship rebase, exploration, comparison or supervisory review.

The current v1.5 definition follows the May 6 ontology–dynamics–regime split. It distinguishes primitive processes, operating conditions and computed behavior labels before downstream transformations.

## Example and boundary

A technician can diagnose a fault; a manager can authorize entry; a building system can unlock the door. Combining those capabilities into “the team can repair it” may hide a necessary permission. Coupling identifies the separate carriers and dependencies.

A candidate system is a proposed interpretation, not a verified causal model. New relationship edges need evidence. Read the [CTR page](CTR.md) next when the system must be transformed, or [CREM](CREM.md) when party relationships are central.
