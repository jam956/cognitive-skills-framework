# DARE/IDCM: question the frame and the discarded alternatives

**Question:** Did the process become coherent by excluding something important?

## The problem and the method

A final answer can be internally consistent because the opening abstraction was too narrow or a troublesome branch was pruned early. A general instruction to “double-check” may review the surviving answer without revisiting how it survived.

DARE audits the path of selection: anchors, assumptions, rejected branches, dependencies and uncertainty. It may request branch rescue, a different operator or bounded suspension of the opening anchor. Reconstitution must then reconnect the revised interpretation to the task and evidence.

IDCM focuses on signatures of missing understanding: residual error, contradiction, fragile conclusions or repeated failed repair. It proposes targeted corrections rather than treating every difficulty as a reason to restart everything.

## Historical development

Recovered May 2, 2026 discussion introduced DARE as a supervisory counteragent and IDCM as a related correction module. The combined v1.0 specification is present in the May 3 baseline. Later discussion distinguished checkpointed audit functions from genuinely parallel orchestration.

The key development was making discarded alternatives and selection grounds available to supervision, rather than judging only final prose.

## Example and boundary

A permission explanation may fit a repair delay, yet fail to explain why authorized repairs also stall. That residue can justify reopening parts availability or another mechanism. It does not establish that any particular hidden cause exists.

The specification describes an external supervisory role. Reading it does not create autonomous parallel agents or reveal a model's unknown knowledge. A requested falsifier is a proposed check until performed.
