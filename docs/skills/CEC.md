# CEC: check whether the path can actually be followed

**Question:** Is each consequential step licensed by what comes before it?

## The problem and the method

A plausible destination can conceal a missing intermediate step. A model may name a cause without the connecting mechanism, propose execution before permission, or present a conclusion that its premises do not establish.

CEC unrolls dependencies, inserts necessary intermediate states, checks forward or backward chains and identifies gaps. Its output distinguishes a supported path from a conditional, incomplete or reconstruction-required path.

## Historical development

Recovered early-April 2026 discussion identified sequential, derivational and path transformations as a requirement beyond exploring conceptual space. A CEC v1 artifact survives in the historical account.

CEC v1.1, carrying May 2 metadata and present in the May 3 baseline, refined this into a conditional late-stage validator. It can be invoked earlier for proofs, causal mechanisms, debugging or execution plans where order is the task itself.

That placement also protects exploration: a novel branch should not be discarded merely because its connecting path has not yet been reconstructed.

## Example and boundary

“Diagnose the fault, enter the workshop, repair the pump” is incomplete when entry requires a separate authorization. CEC makes that dependency visible and distinguishes requesting approval from receiving it.

“Think step by step” can elicit similar work. CEC adds named gap states, dependency checks and a receiving contract. It does not establish external facts or causal truth merely by arranging claims in sequence.
