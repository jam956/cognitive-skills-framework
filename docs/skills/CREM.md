# CREM: reframe relationships without erasing the parties

For the extended treatment, see [Chapter 12: CREM: Reframing Relationships](../chapters/12_RELATIONSHIP_REBASE.md).

**Question:** Can we change the structure of a disagreement while preserving the differences and dependencies that make it real?

## The problem and the method

“Find common ground” can turn incompatible interests into artificial consensus. Choosing a winner or averaging positions can also miss a shared dependency that constrains every party.

CREM separates opposition from alignment, identifies the relationship producing conflict, and looks for a rebase that preserves necessary dependencies. It can support coordination while leaving trust, interests or judgments unresolved. Reframing is useful when it changes the available relationship, not merely the wording.

## Historical development

The early CREM pipeline is evidenced by a source PDF with April 26, 2026 metadata and recovered discussions around April 26–27. It focused on conflict-generating invariants, shared dependencies and edge discovery.

On May 2, the scope expanded beyond two-party conflict to multiple parties, aligned dynamics and relationship strengthening. The v1.3 specification then separated operator conflict from regime conflict. Similar actions under different authority or resource conditions may explain apparent incompatibility.

## Example and boundary

Two groups share infrastructure. One wants autonomy over upgrades; the other wants dependable operating standards. A candidate rebase separates upgrade approval from shared maintenance obligations. It does not assume that either group accepts the proposal or that trust has been restored.

CREM should retain productive tension and party integrity. Its gates are design checks, not proof of a psychological diagnosis or permission to choose for another person. [PSPP](PSPP.md) can compare the resulting arrangements; [CEC](CEC.md) can check dependencies.
