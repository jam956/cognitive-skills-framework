# The Exploration and Transform Engine

Separating access to a notebook's contents from handling the original changes the question. It does not yet tell an archive how to arrange access. Reproductions, supervised consultation and different combinations remain possible. Each needs a more concrete account before it can be compared with the current restrictions.

The **Exploration and Transform Engine**, or **ETE**, develops those alternatives. It applies selected transformations to produce candidate systems: arrangements whose actors, actions, conditions and dependencies can be examined together. Its central question is what the changed view makes possible, and what each possibility requires.

The [circle-and-tilt example](09_THE_CIRCLE_AND_THE_TILT.md) supplied possible directions. ETE now develops the arrangements those movements permit. The [CTR reference guide](../skills/CTR.md) can help name and select a transformation when that choice needs to be explicit. The distinction becomes clearer when one useful insight develops into several materially different arrangements.

## 1. Turn a distinction into candidate regimes

Return to the archive with fragile research notebooks. Its purpose includes meaningful study and preservation for future readers. Some requests concern the text; others may require the paper, ink or binding. Handling is currently restricted. We have not established whether suitable copies or enough supervisory capacity are available.

Four candidate arrangements can now be described:

| Candidate | Proposed arrangement | Consequential limit or dependency |
|---|---|---|
| Current restricted access | Retain the present handling limits. | Determine which research needs they leave unmet. |
| Supervised originals | Serve permitted requests through supervised original consultation. | Requires suitable handling conditions and available supervision. |
| Reproduction-led access | Use copies for routine textual study where their detail is adequate. | Questions requiring the physical object remain unresolved. |
| Tiered access | Use an adequate copy where it serves the question; consider supervised originals where it does not. | Requires a way to distinguish requests and satisfy the relevant conditions. |

*Concretization* gives each opening idea an arrangement. *Recombination* joins needs, carriers and access routes differently. *Comparison* makes the resulting differences visible. The combination produces alternatives that can fail for different reasons, rather than several descriptions of one favored answer.

The current arrangement remains a candidate. A more elaborate proposal has to offer something consequential before it earns further development.

## 2. Vary the dynamicals separately from its conditions

Consider the reproduction-led route. It changes the process by which textual material reaches a reader: consult a copy instead of handling the notebook. Now hold that process steady and vary a condition. A clear, complete reproduction may serve a transcription question; a copy with an unreadable passage may not. The same access route has different applicability under those conditions.

Conversely, hold a requirement steady: a particular request depends on the original binding. Changing the process to ordinary copying does not satisfy that requirement simply because copying worked for another request. Supervised consultation may remain a candidate if the other necessary conditions can be met.

This is **split-layer exploration**. *Decomposition* separates the action from the condition governing its usefulness. *Controlled variation* changes one material feature at a time. *Comparison* shows what that feature changes. Together they help identify whether a candidate needs another process, better evidence about a condition, or a different binding between a request and its evidence.

The earlier prisms remain useful during exploration. Ontology identifies the notebook, copy and research request. Dynamics describe producing, transmitting and consulting material. Regime retains adequacy and handling conditions. Coupling connects the request to a route and its responsible carrier. Changing the arrangement can require revisiting any of these accounts.

## 3. Give each branch a return path

A proposal can become attractive by making its hardest requirement disappear. “Put the notebooks online” sounds complete until a researcher needs information the reproduction cannot convey. “Provide supervised access” sounds complete until the archive lacks the required capacity or the object cannot be handled under acceptable conditions.

The **return path** connects the explored arrangement to the original concern. For each candidate, we can trace which research need it serves, how it serves that need, and how the preservation requirement remains represented. A gap in that trace is useful information about the branch.

*Relation extraction* recovers the dependency between the question and its evidence. *Counterexample construction* supplies a request that could expose the branch's limit. *Evaluation* checks whether the proposed route still answers the opening problem. Their combination gives an alternative more substance than an appealing name such as “digital access” or “balanced policy.”

The tiered arrangement illustrates the point. It combines two routes, but it also adds a decision: which route fits this request? That decision requires enough information about the request and the proposed evidence carrier. A combined branch can introduce a new dependency while resolving an earlier one. ETE keeps that dependency with the candidate so later comparison can assess it.

## 4. Keep useful differences and retain rejection reasons

Exploration can continue indefinitely if every new phrase counts as another branch. “Online copies,” “digital availability” and “remote access” may describe the same arrangement. They become distinct candidates only when a difference in evidence, resources, roles or conditions changes what can be supported.

The supervised-originals proposal may also duplicate the current policy once that policy is described. In that case, the two entries can be joined rather than counted as separate alternatives. A different label supplies no new branch by itself.

ETE therefore bounds the search. A branch earns attention by exposing a different route, a missing dependency, a useful countercase or a condition that changes viability. *Classification* groups variants that perform the same work. *Comparison* preserves material differences. *Selective pruning* sets aside branches whose additional development would repeat what is already known.

A rejected branch keeps its reason. Suppose a reproduction-led proposal is presented as serving every research request. The original-binding countercase defeats that claim. The narrower route may still serve textual requests; rejecting its universal claim need not erase that useful contribution. Likewise, a staffing-dependent branch can remain conditional while availability is unknown.

This distinguishes failure, uncertainty and limited scope. They lead to different next acts: repair a failed relation, obtain missing evidence, or retain a candidate for the requests it can serve. New information can justify reopening a branch that was previously set aside.

## 5. Hand over alternatives that can be compared

ETE has done useful work when a small set of candidates carries enough structure for a meaningful comparison. For the archive, that means retaining the proposed route, the research needs it can serve, the preservation requirement, its dependencies, and what remains unresolved.

*Concretization* has made the opening ideas inspectable, *comparison* has separated their consequences, and *evaluation* has exposed their limits. Their combination adds a candidate set whose differences can justify a choice.

The next question concerns choice. If suitable copies already exist, a reproduction-led route may offer immediate value for some requests. If original consultation is necessary, handling conditions and supervision become decisive. These are grounds for the **PSPP** comparison developed next. ETE supplies candidates and their limitations rather than making the final selection its own job.

The process can also reveal a different kind of problem. If the participants disagree about whose needs or authority an arrangement preserves, a relationship rebase may be needed. If the proposal depends on a consequential sequence, its ordered path may need separate examination. Those discoveries guide the next useful method; they do not require every module to run.

A bounded request can make exploration inspectable:

> Apply ETE to the archive case after distinguishing access to contents from original handling. Develop the current restricted-access baseline, supervised originals, reproduction-led access, and a tiered route. Identify any entries that describe the same arrangement; retain materially distinct candidates. For each, keep the research need, preservation requirement, dependencies and unresolved conditions. Test a textual request and an original-dependent request. Keep rejected claims and their reasons, then hand over a small candidate set for comparison without selecting a winner.

The specification organizes exploration a model or application performs. Its value appears in the candidates it returns: whether they differ in consequential ways, preserve the opening concern, and expose what later evidence or comparison must settle.
