# CREM: Reframing Relationships

The archive's tiered proposal gives different research needs different routes. An adequate copy may serve a wording question; a question about the binding may still require the original. That division can improve the proposal while leaving a disagreement intact. Researchers may find their needs poorly represented, while conservators may find that the proposal assigns them responsibilities without workable handling conditions.

**CREM—the Conflict and Relationship Rebase Module—**examines the relationship producing that difficulty. It separates what each party does from the conditions under which it acts, then develops arrangements that preserve consequential differences and dependencies. Its scope includes strengthening an already compatible relationship as well as addressing conflict. A rebase changes how the parties' requirements relate; its usefulness depends on what that change actually permits.

## 1. Retain the parties before changing their relationship

What must remain recognizable about each perspective after the rebase?

For the archive, begin with the concerns already established. Researchers need evidence adequate to their questions. Conservators preserve fragile notebooks for continued use. A researcher studying wording and one studying the binding may have different evidence requirements, even though both belong to the broad category of researchers.

Those differences matter when a proposal appears to offer everyone “access.” A clear page image may answer the first request. It may leave the second unanswered. Representing both researchers through the successful wording case would make the proposal look more inclusive by losing a material distinction.

CREM therefore maps each coherent perspective separately: the relevant entities, the party's role, its actions, their operating conditions, and the requirement it needs preserved. The parties can be people, groups, institutions or candidate branches. A larger inquiry can retain several relationships rather than reducing the system to two opposing camps.

*Decomposition* separates a broad constituency into consequential perspectives. *Abstraction* identifies the requirement each perspective protects. *Role binding* keeps responsibility attached to the party able or authorized to exercise it. Their combination supplies a relationship that can change without making its participants interchangeable. Unstated motives, consent and trust remain unknown.

## 2. Locate conflict and alignment at the right layer

Does the difficulty concern the actions themselves or the conditions of their use?

An **operator conflict** concerns incompatible action: handling the notebook now and preventing that handling cannot both govern the same event. A **regime conflict** concerns conditions such as timing, resources, authority or acceptable risk. If both parties support supervised consultation in principle but the proposed arrangement requires supervision that is unavailable, the shared action has not supplied compatible conditions.

The distinction also applies to alignment. Researchers and conservators may contribute complementary detection: one identifies a mark consequential to the research, while the other identifies a vulnerability consequential to handling. This is a possible **operator alignment** because their different activities can support the same decision. **Regime alignment** concerns compatible conditions under which they can coordinate, such as an authorized scope of examination and an acceptable handling arrangement.

Shared operators alone are insufficient. Both parties may gate a proposed study: the researcher checks whether its evidence can answer the question; the conservator checks whether its handling can be supported. The common operation does not give them identical criteria or authority.

*Label decomposition* opens descriptions such as “obstruction” or “cooperation” into the actions and conditions supporting them. *Classification* separates opposition, alignment and complementarity at each layer. *Comparison* then identifies what a proposed change must address. This prevents a missing resource from becoming an invented disagreement about purpose, or agreeable language from becoming evidence of workable coordination.

## 3. Rebase around a dependency that survives the change

Which relationship must every useful arrangement preserve?

Both study and conservation depend on evidence remaining available. The binding researcher needs the physical features that make the inquiry possible; preservation keeps those features available for present and future examination. This shared dependency does not make their immediate proposals compatible. It explains why changing the relationship must retain both evidential usefulness and material continuity.

A candidate rebase can separate the choice of evidence carrier from the authorization of original handling. Researchers specify what their questions require. The archive assesses what the available copy conveys and what handling the original can support. An adequate copy can then serve the wording request while the original-dependent request retains its distinct requirements.

The change has a return path to the opening disagreement. Treating every request as original handling coupled meaningful study to a potentially damaging act. Differentiating evidence needs can loosen that dependency for some requests. It does not loosen it for every request or establish that the available copies are adequate.

*Relation extraction* identifies the shared dependency. *Perspective change* moves attention from choosing between parties to organizing their contributions. *Recombination* connects requests, evidence carriers and handling decisions differently. Together they create a candidate arrangement whose consequences can be inspected. A proposed invariant earns its place by explaining the original difficulty and constraining the rebase; a general declaration that everyone values the archive would supply too little guidance.

## 4. Preserve the tension that still carries information

What would apparent agreement conceal?

Suppose the tiered proposal counts a binding request as satisfied by supplying page images. The disagreement can disappear from the report while the evidence requirement remains unmet. Alternatively, assigning conservators responsibility for every requested consultation can conceal unavailable capacity or authority the proposal has never established.

CREM's **party integrity** and **false harmony** checks target these losses. The revised arrangement should preserve the researcher's ability to identify inadequate evidence and the conservator's responsibility to identify unsupported handling. Those judgments can continue to conflict productively. Their persistence may expose the precise condition that further work must resolve.

Alignment can also be developed directly. Where the parties already recognize complementary contributions, a plan might make the relevant research feature and handling concern available in the same review. That is a proposal for better coordination. Whether it improves trust or is accepted requires evidence beyond the proposed structure.

*Countercase construction* tests the rebase with the binding request. *Evaluation* checks whose requirement or burden has disappeared. *Scope preservation* retains the distinction between a useful textual route and the unresolved material route. Their combination adds disciplined relationship change: an arrangement can improve without claiming complete reconciliation. The specification's gates organize that examination; their scores do not establish consent, diagnose a party's psychology or transfer decision authority.

## 5. Hand over a changed arrangement with its grounds

What should the next decision receive from the rebase?

For this case, the useful handoff contains the party distinctions, the operator and regime relationships, the shared evidence dependency, and a small set of candidate arrangements. It also carries the unresolved binding request and the facts needed to assess any original-access route.

The [CREM packet template](../../framework/CREM_v1.3_PacketTemplate.json) keeps these contributions separate. Its rebase plan records preserved dependencies and modified constraints. Candidate branches retain their changes and falsifiers. The gate report records where apparent compatibility might erase a party or hide an asymmetry. These are contracts for reasoning and handoff, rather than descriptions of a model's internal computation.

*Concretization* makes the rebase a proposed division of work. *Comparison* can distinguish that proposal from retaining the current relationship. *Evaluation* preserves the grounds and limits for the receiving method. [PSPP](11_PARALLEL_SYSTEM_COMPARISON.md) can compare the arrangements; [CEC](13_CAUSAL_ENTAILMENT_CHAINING.md) can inspect their consequential dependencies. CREM develops the relationship change without making the final selection its own task.

> Apply CREM to researchers and conservators considering access to fragile notebooks. Preserve the distinct needs of wording and binding research. Separate incompatible actions from incompatible authority, resource or handling conditions; identify any supported operator and regime alignment. Develop a rebase around adequate evidence and continued preservation. Retain each party's responsibilities, unknown permissions, copy adequacy and supervision. Test whether the arrangement hides an unmet need or transfers an unsupported burden. Return candidate arrangements, preserved dependencies and unresolved tensions for comparison. Treat acceptance and improved trust as matters to establish.
