# DARE/IDCM: Reopening the Frame

The archive's tiered access proposal appears to preserve both meaningful study and the fragile notebooks. Use an adequate reproduction for a wording question; consider permitted, supervised original consultation for a question about the binding. Yet a tidy account can conceal a lost requirement. If the binding request has quietly disappeared from the final recommendation, the proposal may sound complete because its difficult case was removed.

**DARE/IDCM—the Discontinuous Anchor Reconstitution Engine / Ignorance Detection and Correction Module—**examines how the answer survived. It reviews organizing anchors, rejected branches, dependencies and unresolved observations. Its contribution is supervisory: identify where the process may have excluded something consequential, then request an appropriate correction.

## 1. Inspect the selection path

A final answer gives only part of the material needed for this review. “Provide digital access” does not show whether the process distinguished textual study from material examination, or why original consultation was set aside.

DARE/IDCM takes the original prompt together with available module packets and decision artifacts. In the archive case, useful artifacts include the research requirements, the candidates considered, pruning reasons, preserved assumptions, verification failures and the grounds for selection. These let an auditor compare what entered the inquiry with what remains in the answer.

Suppose the comparison rejected a reproduction because it could not answer the binding question. That reason supports a particular rejection. It does not establish that the reproduction is useless for reading a passage. Conversely, selecting a copy for the wording request does not resolve the binding request. The scope of each judgment matters.

*Decomposition* separates the recommendation from its supporting decisions. *Comparison* relates those decisions to the original requirements. *Scope preservation* retains which request each finding concerns. Their combination makes an omission visible even when the surviving prose is coherent.

The first check is therefore whether the necessary artifacts exist. Missing pruning reasons cannot be reconstructed confidently from a polished conclusion. DARE/IDCM can request them or qualify its review; an incomplete record remains an incomplete audit surface.

## 2. Reopen a branch for a stated reason

Pruning is necessary when several proposals repeat the same arrangement or fail a governing requirement. DARE/IDCM asks whether the rejection reason was strong enough for what was discarded.

Consider a record that says only “copies inadequate.” If the underlying countercase concerned the binding, the rejected branch may retain value for textual study. Rescue would restore that narrower possibility to comparison, together with the requirement to establish that the actual copy supplies the needed detail.

Different failures call for different corrections:

| Audit finding | Targeted next act |
|---|---|
| A request-specific failure became a universal rejection. | Return the scoped branch to ETE or PSPP for development or reconsideration. |
| Several “different” proposals vary only appointment length. | Ask whether a missing distinction or transformation could produce a materially different route. |
| A proposal promises original access without establishing the necessary order of permissions and handling checks. | Request CEC review of the path. |

*Abstraction* recovers the reason behind a rejection. *Counterexample construction* tests its scope. *Comparison* asks whether retaining the branch could change the result. Together these moves support selective rescue rather than restoring every discarded idea.

Rescue does not make a branch viable. A copy still needs inspection; original access still needs appropriate permission and handling conditions. DARE/IDCM supplies the reason to reconsider, while the responsible solving module develops or compares the candidate.

## 3. Locate missing understanding without inventing it

IDCM treats contradiction, residual error, fragile conclusions and repeated failed repair as reasons to question the adequacy of the current account. Possibly during the course of processing, a key constraint or regime may be lost.  Sometimes it may be a blind spot inherited by the model during training.  DARE/IDCM exists in an attempt to recover the missing constraint.  The specification groups such signals under **unknown-pressure**.

Suppose each proposed repair changes appointment duration, while the unresolved request requires evidence absent from the copy. More scheduling detail has not addressed that difficulty. The useful correction may be to distinguish the evidence carrier from the conditions of consultation. This is an **operator gap**: the inquiry needs a different kind of transformation, such as decomposition or constraint isolation, before repeating the same search.

Unknown-pressure does not reveal an unknown cause. It indicates that the represented problem may be insufficient. The specification supplies weights and thresholds for combining distortion signals; these are design settings, not measurements of an LLM's hidden knowledge or proof that a conclusion is wrong.

The same review attends to **false harmony**. A policy that calls the archive's goals reconciled while erasing original-dependent research has lost one party's requirement. A policy that assumes conservators must accept any handling necessary for research has similarly discarded a consequential boundary. DARE/IDCM can request CREM review of those relationships.

*Relation extraction* recovers the requirement that a proposed agreement concealed. *Negation* asks what would defeat the claim of completeness. *Evaluation* checks whether the missing relation changes the recommendation. Their combination distinguishes an attractive synthesis from one that preserves the concerns it claims to serve.

## 4. Suspend an anchor only with a return to build

A targeted repair is usually preferable to replacing the organizing frame. Correcting “copies are inadequate” to “this copy cannot answer this material question” may be sufficient.

A deeper departure becomes relevant when ordinary repairs remain insufficient and the anchor itself appears to exclude the needed relationship. Imagine an inquiry still organized around “access means original handling.” Every proposal then varies how much handling to allow. Temporarily suspending that assumption permits the alternative anchor developed in the earlier chapters: relate each research question to an evidence carrier capable of serving it.

This departure preserves the notebooks, the stated research needs, the goal of future availability, and the actual permission and conservation constraints. It suspends an assumption about their relationship. It grants no authority to relax those constraints.

The source protocol makes anchor suspension bounded and authorization dependent; the input setting defaults to disallowing it. When authorized, the reconstitution record must explain the old anchor's failure, preserve observations and hard constraints, propose replacement anchors, distinguish surviving from discarded dependencies, and name falsifiers and speculation status. If order matters, CEC review is required.

For the archive, a falsifier is concrete: the proposed evidence carrier omits the feature the researcher needs. The new anchor would then fail to justify that route. *Abstraction* separates the goal from an assumed means; *viewpoint change* permits another arrangement; *concretization* and *evaluation* reconstruct its connection to the task. That controlled departure and return is the added capability of reconstitution.

## 5. Return an audit that changes the next act

The useful output is an audit report and a disposition: proceed, proceed with caveats, or hold for a specified correction. It may carry rescued branches, operator requests or a reconstitution packet. In the archive, it could restore copy-based textual study to comparison while retaining the unmet binding request and its unresolved handling conditions.

DARE/IDCM does not own external fact verification, candidate selection, relationship rebase execution, ordered-path validation or final synthesis. Its requests go to the relevant module or orchestrator; the final Arbiter receives its approval decision and caveats.

The specification describes a parallel supervisory role with checkpoints across the stack. An application can implement that arrangement, or request a bounded audit after a comparison. Reading the JSON does not launch a separate service or simultaneous agents.

A bounded request keeps the supervisory job distinct:

> Audit the archive recommendation against the original research and preservation requirements. Inspect the available anchor, candidate and rejection records. Check whether a request-specific failure became a general rejection, whether missing operators narrowed the search, and whether apparent agreement erased a party's requirement. Request the smallest useful correction. Leave missing artifacts and external facts unresolved. Do not suspend the anchor without authorization; if suspension is authorized and justified, preserve the goal and hard constraints, supply a return path and falsifiers, and request CEC review where order matters. Return an audit decision and any correction requests for final synthesis.
