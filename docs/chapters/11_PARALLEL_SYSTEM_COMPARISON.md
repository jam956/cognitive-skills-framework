# PSPP: Comparing Candidate Systems

The archive has several ways to approach access to its fragile notebooks. It could retain the current restrictions, offer supervised study of originals, use reproductions for suitable questions, or combine those routes according to the request. The [Exploration and Transform Engine](10_EXPLORATION_TRANSFORM_ENGINE.md) made these alternatives available. Their presence leaves a further question: which arrangement is justified by what we know?

The tiered proposal may look attractive because it appears to reconcile access and preservation. It could also depend on copies that omit necessary detail or supervision the archive cannot provide. **PSPP—the Problem Solving Parallel Pipeline—**organizes comparison of candidate systems, keeping their requirements and uncertainties attached to the decision.

The **v5.0-lite** artifact gives that pipeline a focused role: ETE develops the alternatives; PSPP compares what the exploration has produced. In the archive, its parallel structure is the set of alternatives held open for comparison. A model or application carries out the procedure; the JSON specifies no simultaneous-call schedule or worker count.

## 1. Bring the alternatives to the same question

A comparison needs a common concern. Here it is meaningful study of the notebooks while preserving them for future use. Calling one proposal “accessible” and another “protective” does little to establish their relative usefulness. Each arrangement has to be related to the research it would permit and the conditions under which it would operate.

The four candidates can be stated compactly:

| Candidate | What must be examined |
|---|---|
| Current restricted access | What the existing rules actually permit and which requests they leave unmet. |
| Supervised originals | Which original-dependent study can proceed under acceptable handling conditions and available supervision. |
| Reproduction-led access | Which questions the available copies can answer and what evidence they omit. |
| Tiered access | How a request is directed to an adequate copy or to a permitted original-access route. |

The existing arrangement remains a candidate. “Restricted” does not tell us that it blocks all study; it might already include supervised access. If a proposed supervised route merely renames what already happens, it supplies no additional alternative. Conversely, a small change to the present policy may serve the task without a larger redesign.

*Abstraction* identifies the shared concern. *Concretization* supplies what each proposal would do for a particular request. *Comparison* then exposes substantive differences and duplicates. This **normalization** gives the alternatives a common basis without pretending they offer the same service. Each candidate carries more than a name: it identifies who would do what, under which conditions, and how that arrangement serves the original concern.

## 2. Separate a failed requirement from a disadvantage

Suppose a researcher needs to read a passage's wording. A sufficiently clear reproduction may provide the required evidence. Another researcher needs to examine the binding. A copy that reproduces every word can still be inadequate for that second question.

The difference concerns the requirement, rather than a general preference for paper or screens. A reproduction-led route can remain useful for textual study while leaving the binding question unresolved. Treating those judgments as one overall access score would hide the distinction that made the alternatives worth constructing.

Preservation and capacity require the same care. An original-access proposal depends on acceptable handling conditions. If no available handling method meets that requirement, describing the proposal as highly accessible does not make it viable. If safe handling is possible but supervision is scarce, the relevant difficulty may instead be how much study the arrangement can support.

*Decomposition* opens “good access” into the evidence needed, the handling permitted, and the resources required. *Evaluation* checks each candidate against those demands. Their combination separates a branch that fails the present task from one that serves it at a cost or under a remaining condition.

PSPP's specification includes weighted criteria and numerical controls for organizing this comparison. A model-generated number is still a judgment requiring grounds. It cannot make a failed requirement disappear or establish an unknown resource as available.

## 3. Check the difference that could change the choice

An unresolved fact is most useful to investigate when its answer would distinguish the surviving candidates. Repeating that both study and preservation matter adds little once those requirements are shared.

For the wording request, copy adequacy may be decisive. If the available image preserves the relevant marks clearly enough, a copy route can answer the question without original handling for that request. If the marks are unreadable, that route has not supplied the evidence. For the binding request, checking textual legibility again would miss the issue: the inquiry concerns a feature the proposed copy may not represent at all.

This is **differential verification**. The comparison identifies what needs checking; an appropriate inspection supplies the answer. A missing answer remains a condition on the recommendation. It need not become either an invented success or a reason to reject every route.

*Counterfactual variation* asks how the choice changes if a consequential assumption fails. *Falsifier search* makes that failure concrete, such as a requested feature absent from the reproduction. *Comparison* relates the result to the competing arrangements. Together these moves direct effort toward evidence that can change the decision.

The same principle applies to supervision. Its availability matters when an original-access route survives the other requirements. A broad search for staffing improvements is unnecessary if an adequate copy already resolves the current textual request.

## 4. Select, combine, or retain a conditional result

The [tilt](09_THE_CIRCLE_AND_THE_TILT.md#3-tilt-the-frame-by-adding-a-consequential-distinction) made different kinds of access visible. Comparison now examines what each proposed arrangement can support.

Suppose inspection now establishes that the available reproduction answers the wording request, while a conservation assessment finds no suitable original-handling route for the binding request. The comparison can select the reproduction for the first request and defer the second. The selection meets the stated evidence need without original handling; the deferral preserves an unmet need instead of substituting an inadequate copy. Neither judgment rejects copies for every purpose or rules out a future original-access route.

If different requests require different evidence, a tiered arrangement may combine complementary routes. The combination needs a working distinction: use a copy where it supplies the evidence, and consider supervised original access where the question depends on the object and the handling conditions permit it. The original-access requirement remains intact; calling the result a synthesis does not supply missing supervision.

*Recombination* joins contributions that can coexist. *Concretization* binds each contribution to the request it serves. *Evaluation* returns the combined arrangement to the shared requirements. This produces a conditional division of work rather than an average between opposed descriptions.

A model can also defer. If a material question requires original examination and no safe route is established, the useful result may identify that unresolved dependency. The present restriction remains relevant while the archive investigates it. Where two candidates remain plausible, the comparison can retain both and state which missing fact would select between them.

The useful outcome is an account of what the alternatives have earned, including a conditional choice or a clearly located unresolved dependency.

Before relying on a provisional choice, the comparison also asks how it responds to a changed condition. Reduced supervision may narrow a tiered arrangement; a newly identified omission in the copy may reopen a reproduction-led choice. This **fragility check** keeps the recommendation attached to the conditions that support it.

## 5. Keep the grounds available for return

The decision should preserve why a branch was selected, rejected, combined, or left open. “Copies are inadequate” is too broad if the actual finding was that one copy omitted the binding evidence for one request. Retaining that scope allows a different copy or a different research question to reopen the comparison appropriately.

*Abstraction* retains the governing reason; *scope preservation* keeps it attached to the evidence and task. A later *comparison* can then determine whether new circumstances change the result. The added capability is a decision that can be reconsidered without reconstructing the entire inquiry or inheriting an obsolete rejection.

PSPP's selection packet and decision artifacts organize these grounds. They carry the chosen arrangement, alternatives, assumptions, checks, rejection reasons, and remaining uncertainties into the next stage.

| What leaves the comparison | What it carries in the archive example |
|---|---|
| Selected-system packet | Use an adequate reproduction for the wording request; retain the unmet original-examination requirement for the binding request. |
| Decision artifacts | Why the copy route was selected, why the binding request remains unresolved, and which changed facts could reopen either judgment. |

The final synthesis step, called the **Arbiter** in the specification, uses the selected-system packet to compose an answer to the original prompt. PSPP hands over the comparison and its grounds; an appropriate inspection still supplies any external finding on which the answer depends.

**CTR** supplies transformation choices; **ETE** develops alternatives; PSPP compares them. A capable model may use that division selectively within one response or an application may arrange it across separate steps. Together, these transformations help keep ontology, dynamics and regime in context while supporting directed exploration. Branching, pruning and stopping conditions keep attention on alternatives that could change the answer.

Further work follows the unresolved difficulty. **CEC** can examine a consequential order of actions. **CREM** can develop a changed relationship when coherent parties or perspectives still conflict. **DARE/IDCM** can review whether premature pruning concealed a useful route. These methods need not all run because a comparison occurred.

A bounded request keeps the comparison inspectable:

> Compare current restricted access, supervised originals, reproduction-led access, and tiered access for the fragile notebooks. Preserve meaningful study and collection preservation. Distinguish a wording request from a binding request. Leave copy adequacy, current permissions, and supervision unknown unless supplied. Retain the current arrangement unless a material reason rules it out. Identify duplicates, the checks that could change the choice, and any conditional selection or unresolved dependency. Give reasons tied to each request rather than an unsupported overall score. Return a selection packet for final synthesis and a decision record that carries unresolved checks forward.
