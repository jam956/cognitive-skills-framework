# Probe profiles: decide how much machinery the task needs

For the extended treatment, see [Chapter 15: Choosing and Combining the Tools](../../docs/chapters/15_SELECTIVE_TOOL_ROUTING.md).

**Question:** Would a fuller cognitive operation change the next useful decision?

## The problem and the method

Applying every module to every prompt creates context, latency and coordination costs. The framework can become an obstacle when a clear task is forced through elaborate routing and packet production.

Probe-first routing specifies a small initial check. It can suppress a module, emit a compact result, escalate to a fuller operation or change strategy. A short audit stub records why important detail was omitted and what would trigger reconsideration.

## Historical development

Recovered October 20, 2025 discussion describes an earlier lightweight/full probe handshake and a two-phase approach that escalated flagged cases. This is an antecedent, not proof that the current probe-profile file already existed.

The present profiles are in the May 3, 2026 baseline. Recovered May 2 discussion also addresses modular, lazy-loaded routing in response to pipeline size. The May 24 prose-first guide emphasizes natural reasoning with structured handoffs, rather than treating every schema as a mandatory thought form.

## Example and boundary

A direct factual lookup may need no transformation. A contested interpretation may need a provisional KCE anchor and a comparison. Multiple interdependent parties can justify CREM; an execution gap can justify CEC.

The threshold values in the profiles are design settings, not calibrated estimates of expected benefit. The smallest sufficient route is a task judgment that can be wrong and should remain revisable.
