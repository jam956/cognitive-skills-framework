# How the cognitive modules developed

The original [Prism and sentence-thought history](../docs/chapters/03_SENTENCE_THOUGHT_VECTORS.md#1-prism-broaden-the-inquiry-before-closing-it) explains broadening inquiry and preserving a thought's structure. The later modules address what happens next: choosing a transformation, exploring its consequences, comparing candidates, checking relationships and paths, and deciding whether to reopen the frame.

This is a reconstructed design history. Module order is not invention order. Some supporting ideas appeared before the later pipeline acquired its present names.

## The earlier circle and tilt

Before the current registry, executor and comparator acquired separate roles, the Prism/Parallax work used a geometric picture. The inquiry could move around the same subject: develop a thesis, turn toward its antithesis, and retain the paradox when both positions exposed something consequential. A YZ tilt sought a different explanatory basis when revisiting those positions stopped helping.

Recovered December 2025 discussions describe this as preserving the problem's identity and constraints while changing a domain, level, role or explanatory relationship. Other discussions used different descriptions of the Y and Z axes. The functional thread is re-expression with a return path, rather than a single fixed coordinate system or a compulsory sequence of angles.

The [archive example](../docs/chapters/09_THE_CIRCLE_AND_THE_TILT.md) lets the reader follow that movement. Access and preservation remain the concerns throughout; distinguishing kinds of access changes what arrangements become possible.

The later package distributes these jobs. [CTR](../docs/chapters/02_WORKING_WITH_TRANSFORMATIONS.md#reference-examples-and-registry) names rotation, inversion and dimensional lift; [ETE](../docs/chapters/10_EXPLORATION_TRANSFORM_ENGINE.md) explores their consequences, inheriting earlier MPO and MetaDiagnostic responsibilities; [PSPP](../docs/chapters/11_PARALLEL_SYSTEM_COMPARISON.md) compares the resulting candidates. CDP can keep a specified ontology in focus throughout. A tilt does not automatically authorize changing the subject or guarantee a synthesis.

## What this adds to ordinary prompting

An LLM may already perform any of these operations when asked. The framework supplies explicit choices, preservation requirements, handoffs and checks so the operation is easier to repeat and inspect. It remains contextual guidance unless a separate harness implements further control.

The intended advantage over a brief request such as “think broadly,” “find common ground” or “double-check” is greater specificity about what the operation must accomplish. It is not a demonstrated superiority claim over every carefully designed prompt. Models differ in their ability to apply the methods, and full orchestration can cost more than it adds.

That distinction is central to the documentation: a specification describes a method; a worked use illustrates it; a recorded evaluation tests a particular claim. These are different evidence levels.

## What remains to establish

The earliest appearance of every idea has not been recovered. In particular, the precise origins of the current probe-profile contract and the revisions between the 2025 atlas and the 2026 copy remain open. The source package also contains historical numeric controls and instrumentation proposals whose calibration is not established.

The [KCE evaluation](https://github.com/jam956/Key_Concept_Generalization_and_Specification_Eval) supplies recorded outputs and a rubric for selected generalization/specification tasks. It is not a validation of every transformation module. See [evaluation and limits](../docs/appendices/REFERENCE.md#distinguish-illustration-from-evidence) for how to distinguish design intent from tested behavior.
